Mission ON – Smart Choices

A youth drug awareness programme that gets students talking honestly about their lives, with trained near-peer mentors in small, private circles.

What the programme is

Mission ON – Smart Choices treats substance use as something young people often turn to when coping with stress, not only as a knowledge gap. Lectures and street plays raised awareness but did not get children to talk about their own lives. So the programme was rebuilt around trust.

Each session is a play-way, experiential workshop. Students sit in small groups with a mentor who is close to their own age, hear real experiences, and are given a safe space to share theirs.

How a session works

  1. Ice-breaker

    Games and group activities build comfort and a sense of psychological safety.

  2. Storytelling

    Mentors share real experiences using pseudonyms, so students see that these struggles are talked about openly.

  3. Small-group sharing

    Groups of about ten students sit with one mentor. Students share what they are facing, only if they choose to.

  4. Reflection

    Students look at what triggers risky choices, such as academic or home stress, peer pressure and curiosity.

  5. Action plan

    Each school receives a roadmap covering campus safety, access to substances, counselling and parent and community involvement.

What makes it different

Near-peer mentors

Senior students from Pharmacy, Nursing, Dental and Allied Health Sciences at JKKN Institutions lead the circles. They are relatable and trained for the role.

A strict 10:1 ratio

Small groups mean every student is seen and heard, which makes honest conversation possible.

Pseudonyms for everyone

Mentors use pseudonyms, and students’ stories are kept confidential to protect everyone involved.

Monitored sessions

A Program Coordinator monitors all sessions remotely, and mentors work under supervision.

How each session is planned

Before every workshop, the team profiles the institution on two scales so the session fits the audience.

Institution type

A1: private schools with annual fees above ₹1,00,000. A2: private schools below ₹1,00,000. A3: government higher secondary and boys’ schools.

Pre-session risk

Teachers and administrators rate the school as low (B1), moderate (B2) or high (B3) risk. Staff views are often incomplete, because students rarely tell teachers about their own use.
